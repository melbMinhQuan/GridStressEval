\section{Mathematical formulation}

\(N\) customers are divided into small (S), medium (M), and large (L) classes.  Let
\(n_S,n_M,n_L\) denote the number of customers in each class and let
\(I_S,I_M,I_L\) denote their respective current demands.  This means: 
\begin{equation}
    n_S+n_M+n_L=N, \qquad n_S,n_M,n_L\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}.
\end{equation}
Each customer independently belongs to class S, M or L with probabilities
\(p_S,p_M,p_L\), where \(p_S+p_M+p_L=1\) and \(p_j\geq0\).
The random class counts follow a trinomial distribution:
\begin{equation}
    (N_S,N_M,N_L)\sim\operatorname{Trinomial}(N;p_S,p_M,p_L).
\end{equation}
For a composition \(\boldsymbol n=(n_S,n_M,n_L)\), its probability is
\begin{equation}
    w(\boldsymbol n)
    =\Prob(N_S=n_S,N_M=n_M,N_L=n_L)
    =\frac{N!}{n_S!n_M!n_L!}\,p_S^{n_S}p_M^{n_M}p_L^{n_L}.
\end{equation}

And for customer \(i\) in class \(j\in\{S,M,L\}\), define the state
\(X_{j,i}\in\{0,1\}\), where 1 means on and 0 means off.  The aggregate current is:
\begin{equation}
    A=\sum_{j\in\{S,M,L\}} I_j\sum_{i=1}^{n_j}X_{j,i}.
\end{equation}
A violation occurs when the aggregate current is strictly above the network
limit \(T\).  Its probability is:
\begin{equation}
    \Prob(A>T).
\end{equation}
The current study uses:
\begin{equation}
    (I_S,I_M,I_L)=(20,40,100),\qquad N=40,\qquad T=1000.
\end{equation}


\section{All-On model}

Assuming all customer loads are on, every state variable is fixed at
\(X_{j,i}=1\), so the aggregate current for a given composition is:
\begin{equation}
    A_{\mathrm{all}}=I_Sn_S+I_Mn_M+I_Ln_L.
\end{equation}
Consequently, the violation probability for a fixed composition is
\begin{equation}
    \Prob(A>T\mid n_S,n_M,n_L,\text{all on})=
    \begin{cases}
        1, & I_Sn_S+I_Mn_M+I_Ln_L>T,\\
        0, & I_Sn_S+I_Mn_M+I_Ln_L\leq T.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\(T=1000\).

To count the total number of compositions, each composition corresponds to a different placement of the two separators in the array:
\[
    \underbrace{\star\cdots\star}_{n_S}
    \quad\big|\quad
    \underbrace{\star\cdots\star}_{n_M}
    \quad\big|\quad
    \underbrace{\star\cdots\star}_{n_L}
    \qquad .
\]
Choosing the two separator positions among \(N+2\) positions gives
\begin{equation}
    \text{Number of compositions}=\binom{N+2}{2}
    =\frac{(N+2)(N+1)}{2}.
\end{equation}

\section{On--Off model}

For a fixed composition \((n_S,n_M,n_L)\), let \(q_S,q_M,q_L\) be the
respective probabilities that a customer is on.  Customers switch
independently, so the numbers of active customers are independent binomial
random variables:
\begin{equation}
    Y_S\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n_S,q_S),\quad
    Y_M\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n_M,q_M),\quad
    Y_L\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n_L,q_L).
\end{equation}
Here \(n_j\) is the fixed number of customers in class \(j\), whereas
\(Y_j\in\{0,\ldots,n_j\}\) is the random number that are on.
The aggregate current is
\begin{equation}
    A=I_SY_S+I_MY_M+I_LY_L.
\end{equation}
The probability of a violation for this composition is therefore
\begin{equation}
    \Prob(A>T\mid n_S,n_M,n_L)
    =\Prob(I_SY_S+I_MY_M+I_LY_L>T).
\end{equation}
Using independence, this probability can be calculated exactly as
\begin{equation}
    \Prob(A>T\mid n_S,n_M,n_L)
    =\sum_{\substack{y_S=0,\ldots,n_S;\;y_M=0,\ldots,n_M;\;y_L=0,\ldots,n_L\\
                      I_Sy_S+I_My_M+I_Ly_L>T}}
       \prod_{j\in\{S,M,L\}}
       \binom{n_j}{y_j}q_j^{y_j}(1-q_j)^{n_j-y_j}.
\end{equation}

For a population with customer type probabilities \(p_S,p_M,p_L\), the overall
violation probability is
\begin{equation}
    \Prob(A>T)
    =\sum_{\substack{n_S,n_M,n_L\geq0\\n_S+n_M+n_L=N}}
       w(\boldsymbol n)\,
       \Prob(A>T\mid n_S,n_M,n_L).
\end{equation}
